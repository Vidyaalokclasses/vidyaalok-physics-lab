Part 2 · Cube Flux

Cube Flux by Gauss's Law

Place a point charge at the cube centre, at a corner, or at the centre of a face. The 3D construction shows how one cube, two cubes, or eight cubes together enclose the charge, and the flux through one cube follows directly from Gauss’s law.

Centre of cube Corner of cube Face centre Field arrows always visible
Gauss Idea

The total electric flux through a closed surface depends only on the enclosed charge. Symmetry tells us what fraction of that total passes through one cube.

Point Charge
4.0 nC
Flux Through One Cube
q/ε₀

Cube Setup

4.0 nC
\(\Phi_{\text{total}} = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}\)
\(\Phi_{\text{one cube}} = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}\)
\(\Phi_{\text{one cube}} = \frac{4.0\times10^{-9}}{\varepsilon_0}\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}\)

A charge at the centre is fully enclosed by one cube, so that cube gets the full flux.

What To Observe

Centre Case One cube encloses the charge, so \(\Phi_{\text{one cube}}=\frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}\).
Corner Case Eight cubes together enclose the charge, so one cube gets \(\frac{q}{8\varepsilon_0}\).
Face-Centre Case Two cubes together enclose the charge, so one cube gets \(\frac{q}{2\varepsilon_0}\).
